Did your developer meet your expectations for your site?

Lead Developer Report

How did you manage your development and communicate the tasks for your junior devs?

I think I managed my development very well. I created my home page and styled it very quickly which gave me extra time to tweak it before giving it to my junior developers early to allow them to start working on it sooner. I gave my junior developers all of the necessary information in the README file on the home page of our repository and provided information I believed would be useful for them.

How did you communicate with your client? Were there any issues?

To be honest, I did not do a great job communicating with my client. I initially met with them to get their site plan, but I do not think I met with them afterwards. They did not contact me about the site, so I almost forgot I was making the site for a client.

How did you handle publishing your clients site and sharing it with the client?

Publishing the site to GitHub was pretty easy once I set it up. I shared the site URL with my client via email.

How was your experience with managing your site with GitHub?

It was fun being able to manage the site and see all of the pages come together. We did not have any merge issues which was a relief.

Junior Developer Report

How did you communicate with your team lead? Was there any issues?

I had quite a few issues communicating with my lead developers. We had some trouble getting me added to their repositories. When I was added, I was not assigned a page to work on so I had to ask them. This eventually led to a junior developer and I creating the same page as they pushed it before I finished. Luckily the other page was very simple so it was not much of a hassle.

Did you know what was expected of you for each site?

I knew what I was supposed to do for one of my pages as there was a site plan for that page. The other page, however, had a very vague site plan, so I met with the client and they said I should do the same thing that the other page did. I had a better idea once I got the header and footer from my lead developer as well.

Were you able to contribute to your sites?

I was able to create the page I was assigned to while maintaining the style from the site plan.

Did you run into any issues with GutHub and adding your changes to the sites?

The only issue I had with GitHub was when I tried to push my html to GitHub and learned that the page had already been styled and completed, so I almost overrode their changes thinking it was old code written by me.

General Experience and Reflections

What was the most challenging part of this project for you? Were you able to overcome it?

The most difficult part of this project was definitely the initial setup. Most of the porject was spent waiting for someone to add me to their repository or send me their site plan.

What did you enjoy most about this project?

I think being the lead developer was the best part for me because it was the role that gave me the most control of my project. I thought creating the overall style to the site was exciting, and I enjoyed merging everyone's work into one collective project.

What would you do differently if you could do the project again?

I would have the setup for the project be an extra week to make sure that everyone is on the same page. I would also have the lead developers choose at least one junior developer rather than randomly assign them.

Do you feel more confident in working on real-world projects after this experience? Explain.

This project may have been stressful at times, but I know that not an uncommon experience even for professionals. I have learned from work experience that working in groups involves a lot of waiting, so we need to plan ahead and find other things to do while we wait.
